03 · Condition assessment · Kuala Lumpur, Malaysia

Jalan Ampang pedestrian bridge

RoleProject Engineer (part‑time)
SupervisorIr. Parnam Singh, SHRA
PeriodMay 2024 – Feb 2025
StructurePedestrian overbridge

A structural forensic investigation of an in‑service pedestrian bridge over Jalan Ampang, one of Kuala Lumpur's busiest roads, carried out with Structural Healthcare Research Associates. The job was to establish the bridge's current condition and recommend remedial works.

What I did

  • On‑site dimensional survey of the deck, girders, columns and stairs; produced as‑built AutoCAD drawings where none existed.
  • Visual inspection and defect mapping: cracks, spalling, corrosion staining, section loss and drainage issues, each photographed and located on the drawings.
  • Coordinated materials testing and compiled results into the condition appraisal.
  • Drafted the remedial works plan and priorities using the Identify → Classify → Verify → Quantify → Rectify method.
The same workflow — survey, map, classify, prioritise, then plan the fix — is what inspection teams and project engineers do on every rehabilitation job.

01 · Supervised academic bridge inspection · New Haven, Connecticut

Bridge 03017 inspection report

RoleAssistant Team Leader
Course leadDr. Byungik Chang, PE
PeriodOct – Dec 2025
ToolsBluebeam Revu · field forms

A supervised academic inspection of a real Connecticut bridge under Dr. Byungik Chang, PE: review of the previous CTDOT report and sketches, a field visit in PPE, then defect mark‑ups, condition ratings and a written report to FHWA NBIS and CTDOT standards.

What I did

  • Inspected the bridge with the team: reviewed prior CTDOT records, walked the abutments and superstructure, logged defects with photos.
  • Produced the plan and elevation sketches in Bluebeam Revu and located observed defects on them. On Abutment 1: a 1.5‑ft crack between G5 and G6, a 3‑ft crack between G1 and G2, a hollow area 5 ft above the base, a previous patch between G4 and G5, hairline map cracking, silt staining from past joint leakage and efflorescence on the backwall. On Wingwall 1A: a 2.5‑ft efflorescence line 7′5″ above the base, 3.5‑ft vertical efflorescence at the bottom of the cheekwall and 4‑ft efflorescence along the construction joint, with light scaling and missing joint filler.
  • Compiled the final report with condition ratings and repair recommendations to FHWA NBIS and CTDOT standards.

Training behind it

  • FHWA‑NHI 130101/A, 130128 and 130087A completed before and during the course.
